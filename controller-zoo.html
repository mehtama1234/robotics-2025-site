Five ways to control a cart-pole

The same nonlinear plant — a pole on a cart — run under five controllers, from a hand-tuned PID up to sampling MPC. Two tasks: stabilize the pole upright, and the hard one, swing up from hanging. Each controller is really run; each shows its own failure or tradeoff; each links to the 2025 paper that uses it.

One rule runs through the whole family: match the controller to the problem's nonlinearity. Linear feedback (PID, LQR, linear-MPC) stabilizes beautifully near a setpoint but cannot execute a large motion; the swing-up needs the real nonlinear model — solved either by trajectory optimization (iLQR/DDP) or by sampling (MPPI).

PID

classical feedback

A single feedback loop on one signal: push in proportion to the pole's tilt (and its rate). No model, no notion of the cart — just two gains tuned by hand.

It balances the pole for a few seconds, but with no cart feedback the cart accelerates 172 m away and the pole eventually topples (final angle 1.423 rad). And it never swings up.

the tradeoff Single-loop angle feedback can't stabilize an underactuated cart-pole — it doesn't even know the cart exists. This is exactly what full-state feedback (LQR) fixes.

LQR

linear-optimal

Linearize the plant about upright and solve for the optimal linear feedback (the Riccati equation). One matrix gain over the full state — cart and pole together.

Stabilizes upright cleanly (final angle 0.004 rad, cart drift 0.02 m). But the linear model is only valid near upright, so swing-up fails (0.975 rad).

the tradeoff Optimal — but only for the linearization. Far from the setpoint the model is wrong, so it can't handle a large motion like swing-up.

MPC

receding-horizon

Re-solve a short-horizon optimal-control problem every step, so it can respect hard limits (here, a bounded actuator force) that LQR would ignore.

Stabilizes upright within the force limit (final angle 0.005 rad). With a linear model it still can't swing up (2.713 rad).

the tradeoff Adds constraints and re-planning, but a linear-model MPC is still local — the nonlinear swing-up is out of reach.
In the ICRA/IROS 2025 papers Koopman Operator Based Linear Model Predictive Control for Quadruped Trotting

iLQR / DDP

nonlinear trajectory optimization

Iterative LQR: repeatedly linearize the nonlinear dynamics along the current trajectory and improve it with a backward Riccati pass and a forward line search — differential dynamic programming.

Solves the full nonlinear problem: it pumps energy and drives the pole all the way to upright — swing-up succeeds, final angle 0.007 rad (≈0°).

the tradeoff The payoff of using the real nonlinear model. Cost: it needs gradients of the dynamics and a good initialization, and it optimizes one trajectory offline.
In the ICRA/IROS 2025 papers Endpoint-Explicit Differential Dynamic Programming via Exact Resolution

MPPI

sampling MPC

Model-predictive path integral control: each step, sample hundreds of random control sequences, roll them out through the nonlinear model, and take a cost-weighted average — no gradients at all.

Swings up and balances by sampling, closed-loop, with no derivatives — final angle 0.007 rad (≈0°).

the tradeoff Handles any nonlinearity or non-smooth cost, but leans on lots of parallel rollouts and a noise/temperature it is sensitive to.
In the ICRA/IROS 2025 papers Real-Time Whole-Body Control of Legged Robots with Model-Predictive Path Integral Control
