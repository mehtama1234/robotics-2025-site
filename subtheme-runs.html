NavigationTrajectory optimization & MPC
Start from a bad straight-line guess and let the gradient of a smoothness+clearance cost bend the path around the obstacle.
we ran it Collision cost falls 222 → 0.0 over 1,500 gradient steps; the path's closest approach to the obstacle centre rises from 0.09 m to 0.90 m — out to the 0.90 m keep-out radius.
without it The straight-line initialization skims 0.09 m from the obstacle centre, deep inside the 0.90 m keep-out — a collision. Optimization is what makes the path executable.
NavigationSLAM & pose-graph optimization
Add the loop-closure constraint and let least squares spread the accumulated error over the whole trajectory.
we ran it Dead-reckoning drifts to 0.43 m RMS off the true loop; adding one loop-closure edge and solving the pose graph cuts it to 0.06 m — a 7× reduction.
without it Open-loop odometry never returns to start — 0.43 m of unbounded drift. The constraint is what closes the map.
ManipulationGrasp stability & force closure
Pick contacts whose friction cones both contain the line between them; then no external wrench can break the grip.
we ran it With friction μ=0.5, only 29% of random two-finger grasps achieve force closure, versus 100% when the second contact is chosen antipodal to the first.
without it 71% of random grasps sit outside the friction cone — the object slips out the moment it's lifted.
ManipulationVisual servoing (IBVS)
Map the desired pixel change back through the interaction matrix; don't move the camera directly in image space.
we ran it Image error decays 0.578 → 0.0000 in 60 steps through the interaction matrix. The naive 'move toward the pixel error' baseline stalls at 0.212 — it never corrects the depth error.
without it Servoing in raw image space leaves 0.212 residual: it can't tell that half the error is the camera being too far, not too far left.
Control & MPCPID control & the integral term
Proportional feedback alone leaves a steady-state offset against any constant load; the integral term is what drives it to zero.
we ran it Tracking a reference of 1.0 against a constant load, a P-only controller settles at 0.700 — a permanent 0.300 offset. Adding the integral term settles at 1.000 (offset 0.000).
without it P-only leaves 0.300 of steady-state error forever — more gain reduces but never removes it.
LocomotionZMP & balance control
Keep the Zero-Moment Point inside the support polygon: x_zmp = x_com − (z/g)·ẍ must stay within the foot.
we ran it With a 0.9 m CoM and a ±0.10 m foot, the largest CoM acceleration the robot can take before the ZMP reaches the foot edge is 1.09 m/s². A 6 m/s² shove puts the ZMP at -0.55 m — OUTSIDE the foot.
without it At 6 m/s² the ZMP lands at -0.55 m, past the -0.10 m rear edge — the robot rotates about the foot edge and topples.
