drone-flight stack · the pipeline, stage by stage

Flying a delivery

A quadrotor has to know which way is up, plan a path its motors can actually fly, tilt itself to move sideways, turn desired torques into four rotor speeds, and track the plan without lagging. Five stages, five distinct sub-fields — each a plain rule, run and measured, with the failure it prevents. A production narrative, not a grab-bag: each stage feeds the next.

home · the build lab · the field in 22 rules · pick-and-place · navigation stack · driving stack · legged locomotion · warehouse fleet · surgical · agricultural

Each stage is one link: the attitude estimate anchors the controller, the smooth plan gives it something flyable, the tilt law turns motion into rotation, the mixer turns rotation into rotor speeds, and feedforward keeps it all on the line. A weak link anywhere and the drone drifts, jerks, hovers, can't rotate, or lags the path.

1

Know which way is up

Control & MPC

The drone's gyro measures how fast it is rotating — smooth, but integrate it and a tiny bias makes the estimated tilt drift away within seconds. The accelerometer reads the true tilt from gravity, but it is buried in vibration noise. Trust either alone and the attitude estimate is useless.

Fuse a drifting gyro with a noisy accelerometer: the complementary filter keeps the gyro's smoothness without its drift.
attitude RMSE — gyro integration0.38 rad
attitude RMSE — accelerometer0.059 rad
attitude RMSE — complementary filter0.024 rad

Over a rolling maneuver, integrating the 0.05 rad/s-biased gyro drifts to 0.38 rad RMSE; the raw accelerometer sits at 0.059 rad of noise. The complementary filter fusing both holds 0.024 rad.

2

Plan a flyable path

Navigation

The delivery route is a handful of waypoints. Connect them with straight line segments and the drone is asked to change velocity instantly at every corner — an infinite acceleration no motor can deliver. The 'plan' is physically unflyable.

Route through waypoints with a smooth spline; straight segments jump the velocity at every corner, demanding infinite acceleration.
velocity jump at a corner — straight segments2.5 m/s (∞ accel)
velocity jump — smooth spline0.006 m/s
peak acceleration — spline (bounded)6.4 m/s²

Straight segments through the waypoints demand a velocity jump of 2.5 m/s at a corner — an instantaneous, infinite acceleration. The smooth spline keeps velocity continuous (jump 0.006 m/s) with a bounded peak acceleration of 6.4 m/s².

3

Tilt to move

Control & MPC

A quadrotor has four upward rotors and nothing that pushes sideways. A controller that simply commands a horizontal force to chase the target produces exactly zero horizontal motion — the drone hovers in place, forever confused.

A quadrotor is underactuated: to accelerate sideways it must first tilt and let thrust push it; a flat drone goes nowhere.
final position — flat (direct force)0.0 m
final position — tilt control4.94 m
max tilt used31.5°

Asked to move 5.0 m sideways, a flat drone commanding side-force directly ends at 0.0 m — it never budges. Converting the desired acceleration into a tilt (up to 31.5° of roll) carries it to 4.94 m.

4

Mix the motors

Hardware

The controller wants a total lift plus a roll torque. But it cannot command 'torque' — it can only set four rotor speeds. Split the thrust evenly across the rotors and you get the lift but no torque at all, so the drone can never rotate to where it needs to go.

Turn the desired thrust-and-torque wrench into four rotor thrusts with the mixer matrix; an even thrust split produces zero torque.
roll torque — even split0.0 N·m
roll torque — mixer matrix0.5 N·m (commanded 0.5)
rotor thrusts feasibleyes

Commanding a 0.5 N·m roll torque, an even thrust split delivers 0.0 N·m — no rotation. Solving the mixer matrix delivers exactly 0.5 N·m, with all four rotor thrusts feasible (positive).

5

Follow without lag

Control & MPC

With a smooth plan in hand, the drone still has to fly it. A pure feedback controller only reacts to error that has already happened, so on a fast, aggressive trajectory it is always a step behind — the drone lags the path and cuts every curve.

Add the trajectory's own acceleration as feedforward; feedback alone always lags a fast reference.
tracking RMSE — feedback only0.122 m
tracking RMSE — + feedforward0.0139 m
reference frequency3.0 rad/s

Tracking a fast reference (3.0 rad/s), feedback alone lags to 0.122 m RMSE. Adding the trajectory's own acceleration as feedforward cuts the tracking error to 0.0139 m — about 9× tighter.
