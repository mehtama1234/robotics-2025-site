Generative models: learning to make, not just label

A discriminative model draws a boundary; a generative model learns the whole distribution so it can sample new examples. Diffusion, latent spaces, conditioning, and generation for robot action — the engine under synthesis, augmentation, and policy learning — from first principles, drawn live and grounded in the ICRA / IROS / CoRL / RSS and CVPR 2026 corpora.

01 · the problem

Learning to make, not label

Most of supervised learning is discrimination: given an image, predict a label; given a sentence, predict the next word. The model draws a boundary in input space. It has no idea how to make a new image that looks like the training set — only how to sort the ones it sees.

Generative modeling flips the question. Instead of p(label | data), you learn p(data) — a full model of what images, actions, or trajectories look like. Once you have that, you can sample fresh examples from it. That is what opens the door to synthesis, augmentation, and planning by imagination.

the actual math — one level deeper

Discriminative: learn p(y|x) — a boundary. Generative: learn p(x) (or p(x,y)) — the full density.

Sampling a new example = drawing x ~ p(x); evaluation (FID, precision/recall) measures how well the learned density matches the real one.

02 · the engine

Denoise noise into data

The forward pass is easy: take a real example, add a little Gaussian noise, repeat until it is indistinguishable from pure static. No learning needed — it is just arithmetic. The learning problem is the reverse: at each step of that noise process, predict what noise was added, so you can subtract it.

Once you have that denoiser, generation is a loop. Start with pure noise, call the denoiser, step back one tick, call it again — hundreds of steps and you arrive at something that looks like training data. The math is a score function: the gradient of log p(data) tells you which direction makes the sample more probable. Denoising a noisy sample at the right noise level estimates exactly that.

the actual math — one level deeper

Forward: q(xᵗ|xᵗ₋₁) = Ν(xᵗ; √(1-βᵗ)·xᵗ₋₁, βᵗI) — gradually add Gaussian noise over T steps.

Reverse: learn εθ(xᵗ, t) to predict the noise; loss = E[||ε − εθ(xᵗ, t)||²]; sample by iterating xᵗ₋₁ = (xᵗ − predicted noise)/√(1-βᵗ).

03 · compress first

Generate in a smaller space

A VAE encoder squashes the image into a latent vector that is tens of times smaller. The decoder brings it back. Crucially, the latent space is smooth — nearby points decode to similar images — so a diffusion model running there can follow a sensible path without generating visual garbage mid-denoising.

Latent diffusion (the architecture behind Stable Diffusion and its descendants) keeps everything cheap: the expensive U-Net or transformer runs in the tiny latent space, not at full resolution. The decoder only fires once, at the very end. It is the single engineering trick most responsible for bringing high-res generation within reach of a single GPU.

the actual math — one level deeper

Encode: z = E(x); decode: x′ = D(z); the VAE trains both jointly with a KL divergence term to keep the latent smooth.

Latent diffusion runs the score model on z (small), not x (large) — the compute scales with latent dim, not image dim.

04 · steer it

Condition on a prompt

The mechanism is straightforward: feed the prompt as an extra input at every denoising step, and the model learns to denoise conditioned on it. At generation time the same noise is denoised twice — once with the prompt, once without — and you interpolate toward the conditioned direction. Scale that weight up and the output matches the prompt more tightly, at the cost of variety.

That interpolation is classifier-free guidance. The guidance scale is a dial: low means the full distribution, high means a sharp, prompt-faithful sample. The same idea transfers to any modality: condition on a robot action with a camera observation and the model generates the trajectory matching that scene. Conditioning is the tool that turns a sampler into a steerable generator.

the actual math — one level deeper

Conditioned score: εθ(xᵗ, t, c) vs unconditioned: εθ(xᵗ, t, ∅); guidance = εθ(uncond) + w(εθ(cond)−εθ(uncond)).

Guidance scale w trades diversity for fidelity — w=1 is the raw model; w=7–15 is typical for high-fidelity text-to-image.

05 · for robots

Generating the move itself

Diffusion policies treat the action sequence as the thing to generate. The robot’s observation is the conditioning signal. Denoising the action from noise to a clean trajectory samples from the multimodal distribution of what an expert would do — and unlike regression, it does not average conflicting modes into an impossible blend.

World models use the same idea one level up: generate a plausible future observation given the current state and action, then plan by evaluating imagined rollouts. And generative data augmentation uses the model to synthesize whole new training scenes — expanding the data without a camera. One distribution-modeling backbone, three very different uses for robots.

the actual math — one level deeper

Action diffusion: given obs o, denoise aᵗ to a₀ using εθ(aᵗ, t, o); the policy outputs a chunk of T actions, not one.

Multimodality is free: the denoising process samples from the full conditional, not the mean — so it handles multiple valid behaviors without averaging them away.

Part II

The benefits people actually get

Every family below leans on one or more of these — the recurring reasons to model the distribution rather than just draw a boundary.

Sample new plausible data

The model has internalized p(data), so you can draw fresh examples that look real — synthetic images, trajectories, or entire scenes — without collecting more from the world.

Multimodal — hold many valid options

Diffusion and flow models sample from the full distribution, not its mean. When several actions are equally valid, the model produces each; a regressor averages them into an incoherent blend.

One backbone across many modalities

The same denoising architecture works on images, video, robot actions, point clouds, or audio — conditioning changes the target, not the engine.

Controllable via prompt or condition

Text, a reference image, a class label, or a state observation all narrow which part of the distribution you draw from, without retraining the model from scratch.

Generate actions, futures, and training data for robots

Diffusion policies generate action trajectories; world models generate imagined futures; generative augmentation generates synthetic training scenes — the same sampling machinery reused across all three.

01

Diffusion policies

robotics

Treat the action sequence as the thing to generate. Condition the denoiser on the robot observation and let it denoise a trajectory from noise to a clean chunk of behavior — sampling the full multimodal distribution of what an expert would do.

The distribution is the point: when two behaviors are equally valid, diffusion picks one cleanly rather than blending them into a physically impossible average.

  • ROBOProDapt: Proprioceptive Adaptation Using Long-Term Memory Diffusion — diffusion over contact memory — long-horizon state feeds the denoiser for stable, precise manipulation
  • ROBODARE: Diffusion Policy for Autonomous Robot Exploration — adds entropy-based exploration bonuses into the diffusion action distribution
  • ROBOFast Policy Synthesis with Variable Noise Diffusion Models — variance schedule adapts per state to cut denoising steps without quality loss
  • ROBOAdaptive Compliance Policy: Learning to Be Compliant via Diffusion — diffusion-guided compliance — the policy generates the force profile, not just the position
02

Flow matching & trajectory generation

robotics

Flow matching replaces the curved SDE path of diffusion with a straight ODE from noise to data — fewer steps, same quality. For trajectory generation it is especially clean: the flow interpolates between initial and final states.

The multimodal prediction in motion forecasting is the natural fit: learn a flow that maps a single noise sample to one of many plausible future paths.

  • ROBOFlowNav: Flow Matching for Efficient Robot Navigation — flow + depth for goal-conditioned navigation; straight ODE paths cut inference latency
  • ROBOFlowMP: Learning Motion Fields for Efficient Robot Motion Planning — motion fields as the flow target — planning becomes a single ODE solve
  • ROBOTrajFlow: Multi-Modal Trajectory Prediction via Conditional Flow Matching — flow matching over agent futures; diversity comes from different noise seeds
  • ROBOLearning Robotic Manipulation Policies from Point Clouds with Conditional Flow Matching — point-cloud conditioning for 3D manipulation; flow matching fits irregular geometry well
03

Diffusion & denoising models (vision)

vision

On the vision side, diffusion is the backbone behind text-to-image, video synthesis, and editing. The active research questions are speed, controllability, and understanding what the model has actually learned.

Recent work reexamines the assumptions — separating causality from denoising, accelerating via draft-and-refine, and asking whether the standard training signal is optimal.

  • CVPRDRiffusion: Draft-and-Refine Parallel Denoising for High-Quality Image Generation — denoises a coarse draft and a fine residual in parallel — reduces sequential steps without quality loss
  • CVPRDiP: Taming Diffusion Models in Pixel Space — shows diffusion works well directly in pixel space when the architecture is right, without a VAE
  • CVPRCausality in Video Diffusers is Separable from Denoising — finds that temporal causality and denoising are orthogonal properties — guides cleaner architecture choices
  • CVPRLESA: Learnable Stage-Aware Acceleration for Diffusion Models — assigns budget to denoising stages adaptively — bigger steps where the signal is low-frequency, finer where detail forms
04

Flow matching & consistency models (vision)

vision

Straightening the diffusion path and distilling it to few steps are the two main strategies for fast high-quality generation. Flow matching provides the clean ODE; consistency models distill many steps into one.

Bidirectional flow and distribution-matching distillation extend the idea to editing and to matching complex target distributions beyond simple Gaussians.

  • CVPRDelta Rectified Flow Sampling: Inversion-Free Editing via Flow Perturbation — perturbs the flow path to edit images without inversion — no encoder pass needed
  • CVPRBiFM: Bidirectional Flow Matching for Image Editing — maps forward and backward simultaneously so edits are consistent and reversible
  • CVPRPhased Distribution Matching Distillation for Few-Step Synthesis — distills a multi-step diffusion model phase by phase, preserving distribution shape at each stage
  • CVPRFloodDiffusion: Streaming Temporal Motion via Causal Flow — causal flow matching for streaming video — generates frame by frame without storing a full sequence
05

Text-to-image & visual generation

vision

Text-to-image is the highest-visibility application: condition a latent diffusion model on a text embedding and sample an image. The current frontiers are 3D control, multi-condition fusion, and understanding where the models fail.

A benchmark that maps failures is as important as a model that reduces them.

  • CVPRSeeThrough3D: Occlusion-Aware 3D Control for Text-to-Image Diffusion — injects 3D structure as a conditioning signal so the model respects occlusions
  • CVPRDynFusion: Multi-Conditional Fusion for Dynamic Scene Generation — fuses text, depth, and motion conditioning in a single denoising pass
  • CVPRVisionDirector: VLM-Guided Diffusion Refinement — a VLM critiques each sample and feeds corrections back into the denoiser
  • CVPRFailureAtlas: Mapping Text-to-Image Failure Modes — systematic taxonomy of where T2I models fail — wrong counts, spatial relations, attributes
06

Latent diffusion & VAEs

vision

The VAE that compresses image to latent is just as important as the diffusion model that runs there. Better tokenizers and latent spaces directly improve the quality ceiling of everything downstream.

Recent work treats tokenization as a learning problem in its own right — 1D causal tokens, 3D scene tokens, and detail-preserving compression each change what the latent space can express.

  • CVPRDA-VAE: Detail-Aligned Latent Compression for High-Fidelity Image Generation — aligns high-frequency detail between encoder output and decoder target — sharper reconstructions
  • CVPRVision Foundation Models Can Be Good Tokenizers for Image Generation — shows that features from a pretrained vision model make a strong VAE without task-specific design
  • CVPRSceneTok: Learning a 3D Scene Token Space for Generation — maps 3D scenes to discrete tokens — the latent space captures geometry, not just appearance
  • CVPRCaTok: Causal 1D Tokenization for Autoregressive Image Generation — a single causal token sequence replaces 2D patch grids, enabling autoregressive generation
07

Controllable / conditional generation

vision

Moving from text conditioning to fine-grained spatial, layer-wise, or intrinsic-property control is the open problem. The prompt names an object; regional control places it precisely.

Directional attention and intrinsic-decomposition conditioning let the model edit specific properties — reflectance, roughness, lighting — without touching the rest.

  • CVPRLayer-wise Instance Binding for Regional Control in Text-to-Image Generation — binds each text token to a spatial region at each layer — precise multi-object placement
  • CVPRSeeThrough3D: Occlusion-Aware 3D Control for Text-to-Image Diffusion — 3D bounding-box conditioning that propagates depth order through the denoiser
  • CVPRV-RGBX: Intrinsic-Property Video Editing via Decomposed Diffusion — decomposes video into albedo, shading, and roughness; edits one without changing others
  • CVPRDARTS: Directional Attention Routing for Text-Guided Synthesis — routes attention along the prompt’s semantic axes — avoids blending attributes across objects
08

Image & video editing

vision

Editing with a diffusion model means inverting an image back to noise, modifying the trajectory, and denoising again. The practical challenge is doing this without a slow explicit inversion pass — and at video scale, without temporal flicker.

Instruction-based editing scales this to natural-language directives applied across frames.

  • CVPRDelta Rectified Flow Sampling: Inversion-Free Editing via Flow Perturbation — skips inversion by perturbing the flow field directly — one forward pass
  • CVPRCoupled Diffusion Sampling for Consistent Multi-View Editing — shares noise across views during denoising to keep geometry consistent after edits
  • CVPRScaling Instruction-Based Video Editing via Temporal Diffusion — temporal attention + instruction conditioning applied frame-coherently at scale
  • CVPRVIVA: VLM-Guided Video-to-Video Adaptation — a VLM plans the edit; a diffusion model executes it — separating intent from synthesis
09

3D & 4D generation

vision

Generating 3D objects or 4D scenes (3D + time) requires either lifting 2D priors into 3D or learning in 3D-native representations like Gaussians or NeRFs. Feed-forward Gaussian splatting closes the speed gap by predicting splat parameters in a single pass.

Synthesizing arbitrary camera views at scale turns video into a 4D diffusion target: generate any camera trajectory from a set of input frames.

  • CVPRText-Image Conditioned 3D Generation via Triplane Diffusion — diffuses over triplane feature maps — compact 3D representation, image-like denoising
  • CVPRGIFSplat: Feed-Forward Gaussian Splatting from Sparse Views — predicts 3D Gaussian parameters in one forward pass from a few input images — no optimization loop
  • CVPRCatalyst4D: 3D-to-4D Scene Editing via Video Diffusion — lifts static 3D edits to temporally consistent 4D by diffusing in video space
  • CVPRScaling4D: Novel-View Synthesis via 4D Video Diffusion — trains a 4D diffusion model on large video corpora — any camera path at generation time
10

Efficient sampling & distillation

vision

The bottleneck of diffusion is the number of denoising steps: 50–1000 steps is too slow for real-time use. Distillation compresses the trajectory into a few steps by training a student to match the teacher’s multi-step output in one.

Joint distillation and reward alignment (RL on the distilled model) lets the few-step model stay sharp while optimizing for perceptual quality metrics.

  • CVPRFlash-DMD: Few-Step Diffusion with Joint RL Alignment — distills to 4 steps and co-optimizes with a reward model in one training loop
  • CVPRInstantViR: Real-Time Video Inverse Rendering via Distilled Diffusion — distills a video diffusion model to single-step for real-time material estimation
  • CVPRUni-DAD: Unified Distillation and Adaptation for Diffusion Models — one distillation system handles multiple adapters without per-adapter retraining
  • CVPRPhased Distribution Matching Distillation for Few-Step Synthesis — matches the full output distribution phase by phase — fewer artifacts than single-shot distillation

Generative data augmentation for robots

robotics

The easiest way to get more training data for a robot is to generate it. A generative model — Gaussian splatting, diffusion, or a physics sim skin — can produce new scenes, new object placements, or new domain variations without any additional real-world collection.

The constraint is fidelity: synthetic data only helps if it matches the real distribution closely enough that policies trained on it transfer.

  • ROBOSplatSim: Gaussian Splat Simulation for Robot Manipulation Sim-to-Real — renders manipulation scenes as 3D Gaussians — photorealistic sim-to-real transfer without a full physics model
  • ROBOKALIE: Fine-Tuning VLMs on Novel Objects Without Real Robot Data — generates synthetic object images via diffusion and fine-tunes the VLM on those — no robot data needed
  • ROBOSynthDrive: Real-to-Sim-to-Real Assets for Autonomous Driving — diffusion generates synthetic driving assets from real captures and feeds them back as training data
  • ROBODomain Randomization for Object Detection in Manufacturing Environments — randomizes texture, lighting, and pose in a renderer to fill the gap between factory sim and factory floor

World & scene generation

both

A world model generates a plausible next observation given the current state and action — so the agent can plan by imagining rollouts without touching the real environment. Scene generation goes one step further: build entire 3D environments from scratch.

The shared primitive is a generative model of what the world looks like, conditioned on context.

  • ROBOWCDT: World-Centric Diffusion Transformer for Traffic Scene Generation — generates diverse realistic traffic scenes as structured agent trajectories in world coordinates
  • ROBOGraph2Scene: Hierarchical 3D Indoor Scene Generation from Scene Graphs — scene graph as condition; generative model places objects that respect spatial relations
  • ROBODualDiff: Dual-Branch Diffusion for Driving Scene Generation — separate branches for road layout and dynamic agents — consistent joint scene generation
  • CVPRSAGE: Agentic 3D Scene Generation via Multi-Step Generation — an LLM agent orchestrates a sequence of generative calls to build and edit a full 3D scene

Grasp & manipulation synthesis

both

Grasping is a generation problem: given a hand and an object, sample a grasp pose from the distribution of stable, task-appropriate contacts. Diffusion and quality-diversity methods both tackle the multimodality — there are many valid grasps, not one.

Geometry-aware conditioning (mesh normals, part segmentation) focuses the generator on physically plausible contacts.

  • ROBOBODex: Bilevel Grasp Synthesis for Dexterous Manipulation — outer loop selects approach; inner loop optimizes contact points — bilevel generation avoids local minima
  • ROBOQDGset: Quality-Diversity Grasp Dataset for Dexterous Hands — quality-diversity search generates a large, diverse set of valid grasps as training data for learned policies
  • CVPRGeoDexGrasp: Geometry-Aware Dexterous Grasping via Conditional Diffusion — mesh-normal conditioning on the diffusion model guides the denoiser toward geometrically valid grasps
  • CVPRMaskDexGrasp: Part-Aware Grasp Synthesis via Masked Diffusion — masks out non-functional parts during denoising so the model attends to task-relevant contact regions
14

Motion & gesture generation

vision

Generating human motion from a text description is a sequence generation problem over joint angles — the same latent diffusion recipe applies. The challenge is alignment between language and motion: words like ‘walk’ and ‘stride’ are subtly different in joint space.

Large motion datasets and language alignment training let the model generalize to new descriptions without seen examples.

  • CVPRParTY: Part-Guided Text-to-Motion Generation — decomposes motion into body parts and generates each guided by part-level text tokens — finer control
  • CVPROpenT2M: A Large Open-Domain Dataset for Text-to-Motion Generation — large-scale paired text-motion data; shows that coverage matters more than curation for generalization
  • CVPRMoLingo: Motion-Language Alignment via Contrastive Pre-Training — aligns motion and language embeddings before generation — better zero-shot transfer to new verbs
  • CVPRLLaMo: Large Language Model for Human Motion Understanding and Generation — uses an LLM as both a motion encoder and a generator, leveraging language priors for rare motions
15

Autoregressive & sequence generation

both

Autoregressive models factorize the joint distribution into a sequence of conditionals — predict the next token given all previous ones. For images and 3D shapes this means tokenizing the space; for robot actions it means predicting the next move given context.

In-context imitation learning turns this into a robot policy: condition on a few demonstration tokens and the model generates the next action token — no gradient update needed.

  • ROBOICRT: In-Context Imitation via Next-Token Prediction for Robot Learning — few-shot imitation by conditioning an autoregressive model on demonstration tokens — no fine-tuning
  • CVPRShapeAR: Editable Shape Generation via Autoregressive Layered Representation — generates 3D shapes as a sequence of editable layers, each predicted autoregressively
  • CVPRAToken: A Unified Visual Tokenizer for Autoregressive Image Generation — single tokenizer trained jointly on reconstruction and generation objectives for consistent AR modeling
  • CVPRBeyond Patches: Global-Aware Autoregressive Font Generation — global context token prepended to patch sequence — prevents the AR model from losing long-range structure

Part IV

Where it stands

Generative models are the most active area in the field — and several fundamental questions remain genuinely open:

Speed vs quality. Distillation cuts steps but introduces a quality gap. Few-step methods that fully close that gap without per-model retraining are still an active area.
Mode coverage vs consistency. Sampling the full distribution is only useful if the model covers all modes and does not collapse to common ones. Measuring and improving coverage at scale is unsolved.
Control vs creative emergence. Tighter conditioning (spatial, part-level, physics-based) often suppresses the variety that makes generative models useful. Balancing the two is a design question without a clean answer.
Reward and evaluation ambiguity. FID and CLIP score are proxies; what makes a generated action good for a robot is harder to quantify. Evaluation for generative robot policies is an open problem.
Scaling to high-res, 3D, and long video. Compute scales badly with resolution and time horizon. 4D generation at broadcast quality is not yet practical; architectural solutions are still being found.
Distillation quality gap. One- or four-step distilled models still lag their multi-step teachers on fine detail and rare modes. Closing that gap without losing diversity is the central trade-off in efficient sampling.
