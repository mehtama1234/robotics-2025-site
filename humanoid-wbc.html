standing humanoid · whole-body control, stage by stage

Standing tall

A standing humanoid has to do more than not fall over. It balances against a shove, reaches a far target without tipping, moves its arm around its own body, lifts a heavy load without pitching forward, and squeezes an object between two hands without dropping or crushing it. Five stages, four sub-fields — each a plain rule, run and measured, with the failure it prevents.

Each stage is one skill of a humanoid standing in place and working with its hands: hold your balance, reach, don't hit yourself, lift with your whole body, and grip just hard enough.

1

Pick the recovery

Locomotion

Someone shoves the standing humanoid. A small nudge, its ankles alone can push back against. But a real shove gives the center of mass more speed than the ankles can ever cancel — the CoM runs out past the foot and the robot topples, because a fixed ankle-only controller has no bigger move to reach for.

Escalate push recovery from ankle to hip to a step; an ankle-only controller can only absorb a small shove before the CoM runs past the foot and it falls.
max recoverable push — ankle only0.35 m/s (falls)
max recoverable push — escalating1.6 m/s
the test shove1.1 m/s

Ankle-only can absorb a CoM velocity up to 0.35 m/s; the test shove is 1.1 m/s, so ankle-only falls. Escalating through hip (0.75 m/s) to a step (1.6 m/s) catches it — the escalating controller recovers pushes up to 1.6 m/s.

2

Reach without tipping

Manipulation

The hand has to reach a target far out in front. Solve it with the arm alone and the only way to get there is to lean the whole trunk forward — which drags the center of mass out past the front edge of the feet. The hand touches the target, and the robot tips onto its face.

Solve the reach with whole-body IK, not the arm alone; an arm-only reach for a far target leans the trunk and drives the CoM past the support edge, so the robot tips.
CoM excursion — arm only+0.146 m (tips)
CoM excursion — whole-body-0.104 m
support edge0.12 m

Reaching a target 0.55 m out, an arm-only reach puts the CoM at 0.266 m — +0.146 m past the 0.12 m support edge, so it tips. Whole-body IK holds the CoM at 0.016 m (-0.104 m — inside the feet).

3

Don't hit yourself

Manipulation

To move the arm from one pose to another, the simplest plan is to interpolate the joint angles in a straight line. But a straight line in joint space is not a straight line in space — mid-way through the sweep the forearm folds inward and passes clean through the robot's own torso.

Plan the arm motion with a self-collision constraint; a straight joint-space interpolation between two configs can drive the hand and forearm straight through the torso.
min self-clearance — naive interp-0.060 m (hits body)
min self-clearance — constrained+0.020 m
torso radius0.16 m

Against a torso of radius 0.16 m, the naive joint-space interpolation drops the min self-clearance to -0.060 m — negative, the hand is inside the torso. The constrained path keeps it at +0.020 m, clear the whole way.

4

Lift with the body

Control & MPC

The robot picks up a heavy object held out at arm's length. That mass, way out front, drags the combined body-plus-load center of mass forward — past the toes. Nothing is holding the robot up out there, so it pitches forward and falls with the load.

Counter-lean the trunk when lifting a heavy load at arm's length; without it the combined body+load CoM runs past the toes and the robot tips forward.
stability margin — no counter-lean-0.028 m (tips)
stability margin — counter-lean+0.082 m
load22 kg @ 0.55 m

Holding a 22 kg load 0.55 m out, no counter-lean puts the combined CoM at 0.148 m — past the 0.12 m toe edge (-0.028 m margin, tips). Counter-leaning brings it to 0.038 m (+0.082 m inside).

5

Two hands, one object

Safety & guarantees

Two hands carry one object by squeezing it between them. Squeeze too gently and friction can't hold the object's weight — it slips out and drops. Squeeze too hard and you crush it. A naive fixed grip that ignores this band lands on the wrong side of it.

Regulate the two-hand internal grasp force to a band between slip and crush; too little squeeze and friction can't hold the weight so it slips out, too much and it crushes.
grip margin — naive squeeze-15.0 N (slips)
grip margin — regulated+40.0 N
safe band40–120 N

A 40 N object needs at least 40 N of squeeze (friction μ=0.5) and no more than 120 N. A naive 25 N grip has a margin of -15.0 N — negative, it slips. Regulating to 80 N gives +40.0 N — it holds without crushing.
