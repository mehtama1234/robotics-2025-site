00 · The loop everything hangs on

Sense → decide → move, forever, fast

Strip away every subfield and a robot runs one cycle, over and over, in milliseconds: take in the world, choose an action, move a body — then do it again with what changed. Each technique on this page buys one piece of that loop, cheaper or more reliably.

01 · SENSE · depth we ran LightStereo

Distance from two eyes

Two cameras a fixed gap apart see the same point at slightly different horizontal positions. That shift — the disparity — is all you need: near things shift a lot, far things barely move. Depth falls straight out of the geometry.

object depth: —disparity: —depth = focal × baseline / disparity

Mechanism

Triangulate. The same point projects to column uL and uR; their difference is inversely proportional to distance. One subtraction per pixel gives a full depth map.

Why it matters

Depth is the floor everything stands on — and a stereo camera is grams and dollars where a lidar is kilos and thousands.

we ran it → LightStereo on a real KITTI pair: road-foreground warm (near) fading to cool (far), the pedestrian and cars resolved as distinct depths — CPU-fast, on-device.Cheap seeing ↗
02 · SENSE · 3D reconstruction we ran 3D Gaussian Splatting

A scene as a cloud of colored blobs

Don't store the world as a mesh or a grid — store it as millions of little translucent 3D blobs, each with a position, size, and color. Render them, compare to the real photos, and nudge every blob until the renders match. What's left is a 3D scene you can fly a new camera through.

loop: render → compare → nudgeblobs: —splitting where detail is missing

Mechanism

Gradient descent on a blob cloud. Because rendering is differentiable, each photo tells every blob which way to move / recolor. Where error stays high, a blob splits to add detail.

Why it matters

The same blobs are a map, a photoreal renderer, and a physics object at once — one representation many jobs share, fast enough to draw live.

we ran it → the Mip-NeRF360 "garden" scene: ~185 photos → 5.2M blobs → a smooth fly-through of viewpoints the camera never actually visited.Gaussian Splatting ↗
03 · SENSE→DECIDE · efficiency SparseDrive

Spend compute only where something is

The old way scans a dense grid of the whole scene — a hundred thousand cells, most of them empty road and sky. The new way keeps a few dozen tokens, one per real object or lane, and refines only those. Same understanding, a tenth of the work.

dense grid: ~100,000 cellssparse tokens: ~100≈ 10× less compute → runs on the car

Mechanism

Replace the exhaustive grid sweep with a small set of learned queries that attend only to the pixels that matter, carrying detection, tracking and mapping in one pass.

Why it matters

A method that needs a server GPU is useless on a battery. Sparsity is what moves perception from the lab bench onto a moving vehicle.

we ran it → SparseDrive on nuScenes-mini: 6 cameras → detections, tracks, motion forecasts, and a planned "turn right" path — all from ~100 tokens, one forward pass.Self-driving ↗
04 · DECIDE · tracking we ran MCTrack

Which one is which, over time

A single snapshot says "there are cars." A planner needs "that car, and it's heading here." Tracking turns a flicker of noisy, sometimes-missed detections into stable identities: predict where each object should be, match the new detections to it, correct, repeat.

cycle: predict → associate → updateraw detections: noisy + cluttertracks: stable IDs

Mechanism

A Kalman filter predicts each track's next position; Hungarian matching assigns incoming detections to predictions by closeness; unmatched clutter is dropped, gaps are bridged. Identities survive crossings and misses.

Why it matters

You cannot forecast motion or avoid a collision without knowing that this frame's blob and last frame's blob are the same object.

we ran it → MCTrack on a synthesized scene: 267 jittery detections + clutter → 5 clean, ID-consistent tracks at 30 FPS, on a laptop CPU — no GPU.Self-driving ↗
05 · SENSE + MOVE · SLAM we ran Endo-2DTAM

Build the map and locate yourself in it — at once

A chicken-and-egg problem: to know where the camera is you need the map, and to build the map you need to know where the camera is. SLAM solves both together, frame by frame — tracking the pose against the map so far, then extending the map from the new view.

Mechanism

Two coupled estimates feed each other in a loop: pose ← align new frame to map, then map ← add what the new pose reveals. Do it fast enough and the two converge as the camera moves.

Why it matters

It's how a robot — or a surgeon's tool — gets a live, consistent 3D model of a place it has never seen, from nothing but its own moving camera.

we ran it → Endo-2DTAM reconstructed the inside of a colon from endoscope video: dense surface (PSNR 23.5) and recovered 3D geometry — no external tracker, no depth sensor.Medical robots ↗
06 · SENSE · hard conditions we ran SeaSplat

See through a medium that lies

Underwater, the water between camera and reef edits the light: it eats warm colors first (red dies in meters) and adds a blue haze — both worse with distance. The trick is to model that corruption as a function of depth, then divide it back out — separating what the world is from what's between you and it.

observed = true color × attenuation(depth) + backscatter(depth)recover true color by inverting it

Mechanism

Because a 3D reconstruction already knows the distance to every point, it can fit the water's per-depth absorption and haze — the only decomposition that matches all viewpoints at once — then render the scene with the medium removed.

Why it matters

The same idea works for fog, smoke, and murk: scene-vs-medium separation is how you perceive truthfully when the channel itself is corrupting the signal.

we ran it → SeaSplat on a Curaçao reef: the murky blue-green input restored to true warm colors, with far structure that had vanished into the haze brought back.Gaussian Splatting ↗
