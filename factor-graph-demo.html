Factor graphs: one loop-closure fixes the whole trajectory

The maximum-a-posteriori view of SLAM — poses are variables, measurements are factors, and the best map is the least-squares solution that explains them all at once.

home · the build lab · the method zoos · navigation

A pose-graph is a factor graph: each robot pose is a node (variable), and each measurement is a factor — a soft constraint pulling the poses toward agreement. Here a prior anchors the first pose, odometry factors constrain consecutive poses, and one loop-closure factor constrains the last pose to the first when the robot returns. The maximum-a-posteriori estimate of every pose is the nonlinear least-squares solution over all factors; we solve it with Gauss-Newton — linearize each factor's residual, stack the sparse Jacobian, solve the normal equations (JᵀJ)Δx = −Jᵀr, apply the update wrapping angles, and iterate.

we ran itWe drove a robot around a 40 m square loop (45 pose nodes) with biased, noisy odometry, then added one loop-closure factor and one prior. Open-loop, integrating odometry alone, the trajectory drifts and the loop never closes: the end lands 4.07 m from the start and trajectory RMSE vs ground truth is 2.28 m. Nine Gauss-Newton iterations later, the total factor error (χ²) drops from 19,409 to 10.9, the loop-closure gap collapses to 1.6 cm (a 247× reduction) and RMSE falls to 0.39 m (5.9×) — the whole path snaps back onto the true square.
the failure it shows Odometry-only integration drifts without bound and the loop never closes — the robot's estimate of where it ended is metres from where it truly started. The single loop-closure factor doesn't just correct the last pose; the least-squares solve redistributes its error backward over every pose in the loop, so the entire trajectory is pulled into consistency at once.
