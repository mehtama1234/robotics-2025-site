agricultural robot · the field pass, stage by stage

Tending a field

A field robot has to cover every metre, drive the narrow lanes without crushing plants, tell a weed from the crop, hit that weed while rolling, and dose inputs to what each patch actually needs. Five stages, five distinct sub-fields — each a plain rule, run and measured, with the failure it prevents. A production narrative, not a grab-bag: each stage feeds the next.

Each stage is one link in a single field pass: cover it all, drive the rows, see the weed, spray it on the move, and dose to the need. A weak link anywhere and the robot skips ground, crushes seedlings, sprays the crop, misses the weed, or wastes the chemical.

1

Cover the whole field

Navigation

The robot has to treat every square metre of a field — miss a patch and weeds seed it, re-cover a patch and you waste fuel and time. Let it wander toward whatever looks unfinished and it leaves a patchwork of skipped ground and doubled-up passes.

Sweep the field with a systematic boustrophedon path; a naive path leaves gaps and re-covers ground, missing much of the field.
field covered — boustrophedon100%
field covered — naive path28%
field size400 cells

Over the same number of steps on a 400-cell field, the boustrophedon sweep covers 100% of it. A naive wander-to-the-nearest-gap path covers only 28% — most of the field is left untreated.

2

Drive the crop rows

Autonomous driving

The robot must drive the narrow lane between two crop rows without crushing the plants. GPS alone carries a bias of a few tens of centimetres — and when that bias is a big fraction of the row spacing, the robot tracks straight down a row of seedlings.

Centre in the lane from the crop rows you can see, not from GPS; a GPS bias larger than half the row spacing drives the robot onto the plants.
lateral error — GPS0.302 m
lateral error — vision rows0.013 m
row spacing0.75 m

On 0.75 m rows, GPS lane-keeping sits 0.302 m off centre — more than a third of the way to the plants. Vision row-following centres to 0.013 m, comfortably in the lane.

3

Tell crop from weed

Open-vocabulary

To spray weeds and spare the crop, the robot must tell them apart — but both are green plants. Threshold on colour, the obvious signal, and the two are hopelessly mixed: the robot sprays the crop and misses the weed about as often as it gets it right.

Color alone can't separate crop from weed (both are green); add leaf shape and a two-feature boundary tells them apart.
misclassification — colour only46%
misclassification — colour + shape2%

A colour-only threshold misclassifies 46% of plants — barely better than a coin flip, because crop and weed greenness overlap. Adding leaf shape drops the error to 2%.

4

Hit the moving weed

Control & MPC

The robot is rolling forward while it sprays, and the nozzle takes a fraction of a second to fire and for the droplet to land. Trigger the instant the weed is under the nozzle and, in that fraction of a second, the robot has moved on — every shot lands behind the weed and hits bare soil.

Trigger the nozzle early by speed×latency so the spray lands on the moving weed; firing on detection lands every shot behind it.
weed hit rate — fire on detection0%
weed hit rate — lead by latency100%
travel during latency0.3 m

At 1.5 m/s the 0.2 s nozzle latency means the robot travels 0.3 m before the spray lands — far past a 5 cm weed. Firing on detection hits 0%; leading by the latency hits 100%.

5

Dose to the need

Navigation

Fertilizer and chemicals are the biggest running cost, and need varies across a field — some patches are starved, most are fine. Dose the whole field uniformly at a rate high enough for the worst patch and you pour expensive input onto ground that never needed it.

Apply inputs at a variable rate matched to the need map; a uniform dose big enough for the worst cell wastes most of the chemical everywhere else.
input used — uniform dose4441 units
input used — variable rate894 units
input saved80%

To satisfy every one of 900 cells, a uniform dose at the worst cell's rate uses 4441 units of input. Variable-rate application matched to the need map uses 894 — 80% less for the same coverage.
