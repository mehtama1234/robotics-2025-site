the four explainers · one loop

One machine, four models

A robot that does anything in the world runs one tight loop — perceive → imagine → decide → act, then look again. Four deep-dive explainers, one per stage — plus nine more for the systems that make the loop real: the raw seeing and the naming that feed it, the hands, touch, and body that let it act, the way it finds through space, the data it learns from, the road it proves itself on, and the team it becomes when it isn’t one machine but many. This page is what they all add up to.

Pull any embodied-AI system apart and the same circle is turning underneath. It takes in the world as it is, works out what will happen and what to do, moves to do it, and then looks again at what changed. We give the pieces different names and different conferences — but they are one machine, and each of the four explainers takes one arc of it slowly, from first principles.

How the four compose

They are not four topics; they are four handoffs around one loop. Perception builds the scene — and a Gaussian splat is not just a picture, it is an explicit, editable world the next stages can use directly.

That world is exactly what a world model rolls forward: predict how the splat’s objects will move, and you can imagine the consequences of an action before taking it. Several methods literally use 3D Gaussians as the world model’s state.

On top of that understanding, a VLA decides: given the scene and a plain-language goal, reason out what to do. Its weakest spot — precise 3D geometry — is filled by exactly the perception and world-model stages before it, and its output increasingly hands off to…

…a diffusion (or flow) policy, which turns that decision into smooth, committed motion. In fact the newest VLAs simply use a flow-matching action head — the decide and act stages fusing into one. The motion changes the world, the camera sees it, and the loop closes back to perception.

One through-line

Under the four, the same forces push every stage the same way — which is why an advance in one keeps showing up in the others.

Learned, not hand-written.

Every stage replaced a hand-built pipeline (meshes, physics, planners, scripted controllers) with something fit from data.

Foundation-model backbones.

Transformers and web-pretrained vision-language models are the shared tissue — a trick in one field pours straight into the next.

The data & sim-to-real bottleneck.

Real robot data is scarce, so all four lean on web data, generated data, learned simulators, and cross-embodiment sharing — the data-engine explainer is this through-line in full.

Multimodal, and real-time.

Each must hold several valid answers open (grasps, futures, plans, trajectories) and run inside a tight control-loop budget — the field’s constant tension.

Beneath and around the loop

The loop above is the mind. It only works if it has raw seeing and open-vocabulary naming feeding it; hands, a sense of touch, and a body to act with; a way to get where it’s going; data to learn from; a hard place to prove itself; and — more and more — a whole team rather than one. And under all of it run the machinery it learns with, the models it generates from, its grasp of time, the hardware it’s built from, and the clinic where the stakes run highest.
