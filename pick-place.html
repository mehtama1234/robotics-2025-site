pick-and-place · the pipeline, stage by stage

Standing up a pick-and-place cell

The most iconic robot task, walked through end to end. A warehouse arm has to see an object, choose a grasp, reach it, set it down without crushing it, and know it didn't drop it. Five stages, five distinct sub-fields — each a plain rule, run and measured, with the failure it prevents. This is a production narrative, not a grab-bag: every stage feeds the next.

Each stage below is one link in the chain: the object's pose feeds the grasp, the grasp pose feeds the reach, the reach ends in contact, and contact ends in a lift you have to verify. A weak link anywhere drops the object — so each is measured against the failure it prevents.

1 see → 2 grasp → 3 reach → 4 touch → 5 confirm
1

See the object

3D perception

The arm gets a point cloud of the bin: the object and the tabletop it sits on, mixed together. Estimate a grasp pose straight from that cloud and the wide, flat table dominates every statistic — the arm reaches for a pose that is mostly the table's geometry, not the object's.

Segment the object off the table before estimating its pose, or the table's geometry dominates the answer.
yaw error — segment first2.0°
yaw error — raw cloud45.0°
centre error — segment vs raw0.1 cm vs 11 cm

With RANSAC segmentation the estimated yaw is off by 2.0° and the centre by 0.1 cm. Skip it and run the estimate on the raw cloud and the yaw error jumps to 45.0°, the centre to 11 cm — a grasp aimed at the middle of the table.

2

Pick a grasp

Manipulation

Many two-finger grasps 'close' on the object, but most of them sit at the edge of the friction cone and slip the instant the arm lifts or the object is nudged. Grabbing the first one that closes is how objects end up on the floor.

Score every candidate grasp by its friction-cone margin and pick the sturdiest, not just any grasp that closes.
best grasp margin26.2°
median grasp margin-15.0° (slips)
grasps that even close32%

Across 400 sampled grasps (friction cone 26.6°), the median grasp has a margin of -15.0° — negative, it slips — and only 32% even achieve force closure. Scoring and picking the best yields a 26.2° margin, essentially the full cone.

3

Reach it

Manipulation

To place the grasp point where the object is, the arm must turn a target in space into joint angles. When the target sits near the edge of the arm's reach the arm stretches nearly straight — a singularity — and the naive solution commands the joints to spin at impossible speeds.

Near a singularity, damp the inverse — a plain Jacobian inverse commands impossible joint speeds.
peak joint step — naive inverse21.6 rad
peak joint step — damped0.19 rad
final reach error (both)~5 cm at the boundary

Chasing a target 3.05 units out (just past the 3-unit reach), both methods settle at the boundary (~5 cm). But the naive Jacobian inverse commands a peak joint step of 21.6 rad — a violent flail — while damped least squares stays at 0.19 rad, ~114× smoother.

4

Touch down softly

Control & MPC

When the gripper sets the object down, its position command overshoots the real surface by a couple of centimetres — measurement is never exact. Under stiff position control that tiny overshoot becomes a large force, and the arm crushes the part into the table.

Command a compliant force on contact, not a stiff position — so a small position error can't become a large force.
peak contact force — stiff108.4 N
peak contact force — impedance4.0 N
commanded overshoot (both)2 cm

Commanded 2 cm past the surface, a stiff position controller spikes the contact force to 108.4 N — enough to damage the part. A low-stiffness impedance controller with the identical overshoot tops out at 4.0 N.

5

Confirm the grab

Tactile

The grasp looked good, the arm lifts — and somewhere on the way the object slips free. Without a check, the arm completes the whole place motion with an empty gripper and reports success. A silent failure is the worst kind.

Watch the grip force after you close; a silent slip becomes a dropped object you never noticed.
slips caught (recall)100%
false alarms on good grasps0%
lead time before full drop6 steps

Monitoring the grip force (expected 5 N) against a 3.5 N threshold catches 100% of slips at a 0% false-alarm rate, and fires on average 6 steps before the object is fully lost.
