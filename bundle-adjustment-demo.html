Bundle adjustment: refine the whole 3D reconstruction at once

The single global step at the heart of every SfM pipeline and visual SLAM back-end — jointly refine every 3-D point and every camera pose to minimize total reprojection error.

home · the build lab · the method zoos · 3D perception

A reconstruction front-end hands you noisy camera poses and per-view triangulated 3-D points, each carrying error. Bundle adjustment is the joint least-squares refinement that treats every 3-D point and every camera pose as unknowns and slides them all together to minimize the total reprojection error — the pixel gap between where each point lands in each image and where it was actually observed. We solve it with Levenberg-Marquardt on the stacked reprojection residuals, holding one camera fixed to anchor the 7-dof gauge (global rotation, translation, scale).

we ran itWe built 60 3-D points seen by 5 pinhole cameras, added 0.3 px observation noise, then perturbed the camera poses (~1.7° rotation, 15 cm translation) as a noisy front-end would. With those noisy poses plus a linear DLT triangulation, mean reprojection error was 13.27 px. After bundle adjustment it dropped to 0.29 px — a 46x reduction, sub-pixel. The 3-D points moved from 0.46 m to 0.10 m mean error, and per-camera pose error fell from 2.53° to 0.08° rotation and 0.25 m to 0.06 m translation.
the failure it shows With noisy initial poses, no amount of per-view triangulation helps: each camera's error corrupts every point it sees, so the reconstruction stays smeared and the reprojection error is many pixels. Only the joint optimization — refining points and poses together against all observations at once — can drive it to sub-pixel.
