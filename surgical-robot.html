Operating through a robot

A surgeon at a console drives tools inside a patient through tiny incisions. Between their hands and the tissue sit five safeguards — steady the tremor, scale for precision, pivot at the incision, guard the no-go zones, and beat the cable slack — each a plain rule, run and measured, with the failure it prevents. A production narrative, not a grab-bag: each stage feeds the next.

Each stage is one link between the surgeon's intent and a safe cut: filter the hand, shrink the motion, respect the incision, fence off the danger, and take up the slack. A weak link anywhere and the tool shakes, misses, tears, strays, or lags.

1

Steady the hand

Control & MPC

Even a great surgeon's hand carries a fine physiological tremor — a few tenths of a millimetre shaking at around ten times a second. Pass the raw hand command straight to the tool and that shake is amplified right at the tip, where a tenth of a millimetre matters.

Low-pass the command to strip physiological hand tremor while passing the surgeon's deliberate motion.
tremor at the tool — raw0.446 mm
tremor at the tool — filtered0.034 mm
deliberate motion — kept5.02 → 5.14 mm

The 10 Hz tremor at the tool drops from 0.446 mm raw to 0.034 mm after a 3 Hz low-pass — a 13× reduction — while the deliberate 0.5 Hz motion passes untouched (5.02→5.14 mm).

2

Scale the motion down

Medical

The task is sub-millimetre — suturing a vessel a few millimetres across — but the human hand can only hold a position to about a millimetre. Map the hand to the tool one-to-one and the surgeon simply cannot place the tip finely enough, no matter how careful.

Scale the surgeon's hand motion down at the tool; 1:1 hand jitter is far larger than the sub-millimetre task demands.
tool targeting error — 1:10.799 mm
tool targeting error — 5:10.158 mm
hand jitter (input)1.0 mm

With ~1.0 mm of natural hand jitter, a 1:1 mapping lands the tool 0.799 mm from target on average. Scaling 5:1 at the tool cuts that to 0.158 mm — into the sub-millimetre range the task needs.

3

Pivot at the incision

Medical

The tool enters the body through a single small incision, and that incision must not move — dragging the shaft sideways through it tears the tissue. But to reach different spots inside, the tip has to sweep, and a naive Cartesian controller moves the whole shaft to do it.

Constrain the tool to pivot about the fixed incision (remote center of motion); a free Cartesian move drags the shaft through the incision and tears it.
incision displacement — naive8.0 mm (tears)
incision displacement — RCM0.0 mm
tip sweep at depth16 mm @ 50 mm

Sweeping the tip 16 mm across at 50 mm depth, a naive Cartesian move drags the incision point 8.0 mm — tearing it. The remote-center-of-motion constraint holds the incision at 0.0 mm.

4

Guard the no-go zone

Safety & guarantees

Millimetres from the work sits something the tool must never touch — a major vessel, a nerve. The surgeon is focused on the task, and one slip of the command sends the tool straight into the structure that ends the operation badly.

Enforce a virtual fixture around no-go structures; without it the tool follows the command straight into the critical anatomy.
penetration — no fixture3.97 mm
penetration — virtual fixture0.0 mm
keep-out radius4 mm

A command aimed straight through a 4 mm keep-out around a vessel penetrates it by 3.97 mm with no fixture. The virtual fixture holds penetration to 0.0 mm — the tool never enters.

5

Beat the cable slack

Hardware

The wrist at the tool tip is driven by cables running down the shaft, and cables have slack. Every time the surgeon reverses direction, the tip sits still through the backlash before it catches up — the tool feels loose and lags exactly when fine, back-and-forth suturing needs it most.

Feed the cable backlash forward on every reversal; an uncompensated tendon-driven tool lags its command through the deadband.
tip error — uncompensated0.163 mm
tip error — backlash-compensated0.0 mm
cable backlash2.0°

With a 2.0° cable backlash on a 10 mm tool, an uncompensated tip lags its command by 0.163 mm RMS through the reversals. Feeding the backlash forward cuts the tip error to 0.0 mm.
