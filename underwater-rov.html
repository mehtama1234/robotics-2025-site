underwater ROV · the dive, stage by stage

Working the seabed

A remotely-operated vehicle has to hover neutrally in the water, stop on a dock through water that resists like treacle, hold a line across a current, skim a rising seabed without hitting it, and work a manipulator from a base that floats free. Five stages, five ocean-specific sub-fields — each a plain rule, run and measured, with the failure it prevents.

Each stage is one link in the dive: hang neutral, stop against the water, hold the line, fly the bottom, and steady the arm. A weak link anywhere and the vehicle bolts up, overshoots, drifts off, crashes, or misses the grab.

1

Trim to neutral

Hardware

An untrimmed vehicle is either a cork or a rock. If it floats even a little, it burns a vertical thruster the whole dive just holding depth, and the instant that thruster idles it bolts for the surface — dragging the tether and abandoning the worksite.

Trim ballast to neutral buoyancy; an untrimmed vehicle burns a thruster fighting its own float all mission and shoots to the surface the moment it idles.
hold-depth thrust — untrimmed20 N
hold-depth thrust — trimmed0.4 N
idle drift — untrimmed vs trimmed0.63 vs 0.09 m/s

An untrimmed vehicle needs 20 N of constant thruster just to hold depth and drifts up at 0.63 m/s when idle. Trimmed to neutral it needs 0.4 N and idles at 0.09 m/s.

2

Brake for the water

Control & MPC

Underwater, a moving vehicle drags a slug of entrained water with it — the 'added mass' can nearly double the effective inertia. Plan a docking approach with the vehicle's dry mass and it under-brakes, sailing past the docking cone and into the structure.

Brake against the effective mass including entrained-water added mass; a plan using the dry mass under-brakes and overshoots the dock.
docking overshoot — dry mass0.38 m
docking overshoot — added mass0.0 m
dry / added mass50 / 45 kg

Approaching a dock at 1.0 m/s with 50 kg dry and 45 kg of added mass, a dry-mass braking plan overshoots by 0.38 m. Planning with the effective mass overshoots 0.0 m.

3

Crab into the current

Navigation

A steady current runs across the transit line. Point the vehicle straight at the waypoint and the current sweeps it sideways the whole way, so it arrives well off the mark — or fights a long, fuel-burning curve back onto the line.

Crab upstream by asin(current/speed) so the resultant track holds the line; pointing straight at the goal lets the current sweep you off course.
cross-track — aim straight6.0 m
cross-track — crab upstream0.0 m
crab angle17.5°

On a 20 m transit at 1.0 m/s across a 0.3 m/s current, aiming straight at the goal arrives 6.0 m off track. Crabbing 17.5° upstream arrives 0.0 m off.

4

Fly the seabed

Navigation

Surveying near the bottom, the vehicle holds a set depth below the surface. But the seabed is not flat — where it rises toward the vehicle, a constant-depth transit closes the gap without noticing and drives the vehicle straight into the ground.

Follow the seabed by holding altitude above it; a constant-depth transit flies straight into ground that rises toward the vehicle.
min clearance — constant depth-1.0 m (crash)
min clearance — altitude hold2.0 m
target clearance2 m

Over a seabed that rises 3 m, a constant-depth transit closes to -1.0 m clearance — it crashes. Altitude-hold on a 2 m target keeps the minimum clearance at 2.0 m.

5

Steady the arm

Manipulation

The vehicle floats free — nothing bolts it down. Reach out with the manipulator and, by conservation of momentum, the arm's motion shoves the whole vehicle backward. The hand ends up somewhere the vehicle never intended, and the grab misses.

Counter the arm's reaction on the free-floating base; moving the arm on an unanchored vehicle pushes the whole base back and the end-effector misses.
end-effector error — uncompensated0.07 m
end-effector error — compensated0.006 m
base / arm mass200 / 15 kg

Reaching the arm 1 m on a 200 kg free-floating base, the uncompensated reaction shifts the base and lands the end-effector 0.07 m off target. Countering the reaction with thrusters cuts it to 0.006 m.
