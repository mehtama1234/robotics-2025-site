legged-locomotion stack · the gait cycle, stage by stage

Walking on four legs

A quadruped has to feel each footfall, decide where to plant the next foot, stay balanced while a leg is in the air, swing that leg clear of the ground, and share the load across the stance legs without slipping. Five stages of one gait cycle, five distinct sub-fields — each a plain rule, run and measured, with the failure it prevents. A production narrative, not a grab-bag: each stage feeds the next.

Each stage is one link in the cycle: the contact clock times the gait, the foothold sets the speed, the balance check licenses the lift, the swing carries the leg over, and the force split keeps every foot gripping. A weak link anywhere and the robot mistimes, stalls, tips, stubs, or slips.

1

Feel the ground

Tactile

The gait clock depends on knowing the instant each foot touches down and lifts off. Read the foot-force sensor against a single threshold and, as the noisy signal crosses that line, the leg reports a burst of false touchdowns and lift-offs — the gait controller sees the foot flickering on and off.

Detect foot contact with hysteresis, not a single threshold; a bare threshold chatters false touchdowns on sensor noise.
contact transitions — single threshold58 (chatter)
contact transitions — hysteresis10
true transitions8

Across four steps (8 true transitions), a single threshold fires 58 contact transitions as noise chatters across the line. Hysteresis reports 10 — one clean event per real touchdown and lift-off.

2

Place the next foot

Locomotion

The robot is walking at half the speed you asked for and needs to speed up. Where it plants the next foot decides that. Land it straight under the hip every stride and the robot just keeps whatever speed it already had — it never reaches the target.

Place the foot with a speed-correcting offset (Raibert): without the (v−v_des) term the robot walks forever at its starting speed.
final speed — Raibert foothold1.2 m/s
final speed — fixed foothold0.5 m/s
strides to reach target7

Starting at 0.5 m/s with a target of 1.2 m/s, the Raibert foothold reaches the target in 7 strides (1.2 m/s). Dropping the speed-correction term, the robot stays at 0.5 m/s forever.

3

Stay upright

Locomotion

To swing a leg the robot must stand on the other three, and their footprints form a support triangle. Lift the wrong foot while the body's weight is leaning the wrong way and the centre of mass falls outside that triangle — the robot tips before the swing even starts.

Lift only a foot that leaves the CoM inside the remaining support triangle; lift the wrong one and the robot tips.
margin — lift FR (wrong)-0.166 m (tips)
margin — lift RL (right)+0.1 m (stable)
stability ruleCoM inside the support triangle

With the CoM leaning toward the front-right, lifting the FR foot leaves a margin of -0.166 m — negative, the robot tips. Lifting the RL foot instead keeps a +0.1 m margin — stable.

4

Swing the leg over

Locomotion

The swing leg has to travel from where it lifted off to where it will land. Interpolate that in a straight line and the foot skims along at ground level the whole way — the first pebble, kerb, or uneven tile it meets, it stubs and trips the robot.

Arc the swing foot to a clearance apex; a straight-line swing skims the ground and stubs on any bump.
clearance over bump — straight swing-0.05 m (stubs)
clearance over bump — arced swing+0.043 m
swing apex height10 cm

Crossing a 5 cm bump mid-stride, a straight-line swing clears it by -0.05 m — negative, it stubs. An arc to a 10 cm apex clears it by +0.043 m.

5

Push with the stance legs

Control & MPC

The stance legs together must support the body weight and resist a horizontal push. Split that load evenly across all four feet and, when some feet are on a low-traction patch (ice, wet tile), those feet are asked for more grip than they have — they slip and the robot slides.

Distribute stance forces so no foot exceeds its friction cone; an even split overloads the low-traction feet and they slip.
max friction use — even split1.2× (slips)
max friction use — distributed0.35× (holds)
front / rear frictionμ 0.25 / 0.9

Resisting a horizontal push (60 N against 200 N of weight) with the front feet on ice (μ 0.25) and rear on grip (μ 0.9), an even split drives friction use to 1.2× the limit — the icy feet slip. Distributing the force keeps the worst foot at 0.35× — all hold.
