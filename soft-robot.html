Reaching soft

A soft, continuum arm has no rigid joints — it bends into arcs to reach. To do one reach-and-grasp through clutter it has to model its own bend, calibrate the actuator that drives it, conform through a gap a rigid arm cannot use, hold its shape under a payload, and modulate its stiffness for safe contact. Five stages, five distinct sub-fields — each a plain rule, run and measured, with the failure it prevents.

Each stage is one link in the reach cycle: know where the bent tip is, know what the actuator does, bend through the clutter, hold the tip up under load, and stiffen or soften on demand.

1 model the bend → 2 calibrate → 3 conform → 4 hold the load → 5 stiffen
1

Model the bend

3D perception

A soft arm has no rigid joints — it bends into a smooth arc. If you keep the rigid-robot habit of treating each segment as a straight rod pointing along its base, you have no idea where the tip actually is. At a hard bend that error is not millimetres — it is a fifth of the whole segment.

Model a soft segment as a constant-curvature arc, not a rigid rod; a straight-rod model mislocates the tip of a bent arm by tens of centimetres.
tip error — rigid rod22 cm
tip error — PCC arc0.0 cm
bend90°

On a 0.3 m segment bent to 90°, a rigid straight-rod model misplaces the tip by 22 cm. The constant-curvature arc model reproduces the true tip to 0.0 cm.

2

Calibrate the actuator

Hardware

You bend the arm by pushing air into a pneumatic chamber, and you need to know how much curvature a given pressure buys. But the chamber stiffens as it inflates — double the pressure does not double the bend. Fit a straight line through that and every commanded shape is off.

Calibrate pressure-to-curvature with the true nonlinear map; a straight-line fit leaves a systematic curvature error because the actuator saturates as it inflates.
curvature RMS — linear fit0.0374/m
curvature RMS — nonlinear fit0.0018/m
samples400

Over 400 samples, a linear pressure→curvature fit leaves a curvature RMS error of 0.0374/m (3% of range). The nonlinear fit cuts it to 0.0018/m (0%).

3

Let it conform

Manipulation

The reach target sits on the far side of a cluttered field — pipes, cables, edges. A rigid arm can only reach it if there is a clear straight corridor from base to target, and in real clutter there almost never is, so it is blocked most of the time.

Bend around obstacles instead of pushing straight through; a rigid arm needs a clear straight corridor that clutter rarely leaves, while a soft arm conforms and reaches.
reach success — rigid straight arm16%
reach success — soft bending arm100%
obstacles / trial14

Over 400 cluttered trials (14 obstacles each), a rigid straight arm reaches the target just 16% of the time — it needs a clear corridor. The soft bending arm reaches 100%.

4

Hold up the load

Control & MPC

Pick something up and the soft arm sags — with no rigid skeleton, the payload bends the whole cantilever down and the tip lands low. Command the shape you wanted for an unloaded arm and the grasp misses under the weight of what it is carrying.

Feedforward a pre-bend equal to the predicted tip droop; an uncompensated soft cantilever sags under its payload and lands the tip low by the full droop.
tip error — uncompensated37 cm
tip error — feedforward pre-bend0.00 cm
predicted droop37 cm

Under a 4.9 N tip load, an uncompensated soft cantilever droops 37 cm and lands the tip 37 cm low. A feedforward pre-bend puts the tip back on target to 0.00 cm.

5

Stiffen when needed

Control & MPC

The whole point of a soft arm is safe contact — but a fixed-stiff arm driving into an obstacle spikes a large impact force before the controller can even react. Make it permanently soft, though, and it wobbles and loses precision in free space. You need both.

Modulate stiffness: soften on contact to bound the peak force, stay stiff in free space for precision; a fixed-stiff arm spikes a large impact force on every touch.
peak contact force — fixed-stiff24 N
peak contact force — variable-stiffness1 N
free-space deflection — stiff mode0.25 mm

On the same drive-in after touch, a fixed-stiff arm (8000 N/m) spikes a peak contact force of 24 N. A variable-stiffness arm softens to 400 N/m on contact and peaks at just 1 N — while its stiff mode holds free-space deflection to 0.25 mm.
