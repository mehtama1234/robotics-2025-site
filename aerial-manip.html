Grabbing in flight

A flying manipulator that trims for its arm, rejects the arm's reaction, settles in ground effect, pushes stably, and holds a catch. Five stages a quadrotor-with-a-gripper runs to grab a payload mid-air — each a plain rule, run and measured, with the failure it prevents.

Each stage is one link in the grab: trim for where the arm puts the mass, reject the torque the arm kicks back, settle through the ground cushion, push without being thrown off, and re-trim the instant the gripper closes.

1

Trim for the reach

Control & MPC

The moment the arm reaches out to line up a grab, the whole aircraft's center of mass slides toward it. Split the rotor thrusts evenly, as if nothing changed, and the weight now pulls through a point that is off-center from the rotors — the aircraft carries a standing moment it never asked for, and it tips out of hover.

Trim the rotor thrusts for the arm's CoM offset; an equal thrust split leaves a net moment equal to weight times the offset, and the aircraft tips.
residual moment — equal split1.96 N·m (tips)
residual moment — reallocated0.0 N·m
CoM offset from the reach8 cm

With the arm reaching 0.5 m, the CoM shifts 8 cm. An equal 23.5 N thrust split leaves a net moment of 1.96 N·m — the aircraft tips. Reallocating (7.85 N / 15.7 N) drives it to 0.0 N·m.

2

Reject the arm's kick

Control & MPC

To swing the arm into position, a motor accelerates it — and by Newton's third law that same torque kicks back on the body. The body is lighter than you'd hope, so an uncompensated swing rolls the whole aircraft over by several degrees mid-flight, exactly when it needs to be steady to grab.

Feedforward the arm's reaction torque onto the rotors; swinging the arm kicks the lighter body, and left uncompensated the aircraft tilts.
peak body tilt — uncompensated4.3°
peak body tilt — feedforward0.0°
arm angular accel12 rad/s²

An arm swing at 12 rad/s² reacting on a body inertia of 0.02 tilts the aircraft by 4.3° uncompensated. Feeding the reaction forward holds the peak tilt to 0.0°.

3

Settle near the surface

Control & MPC

To grab something off a table the drone has to descend to within a rotor-radius of the surface — and right there the rotors ride on their own reflected downwash. The same thrust that hovered in free air now produces more lift, so a drone holding its hover thrust drifts upward and simply cannot settle to the grab height.

Compensate thrust for ground effect near a surface; holding the free-air hover thrust makes the drone rise on its own cushion and it can't settle to the grab height.
altitude error — hold thrust0.429 m (rises)
altitude error — compensated0.0 m
grab height20 cm

Trying to hold 20 cm above the surface with rotor radius 0.15 m, holding the free-air hover thrust drifts the drone up by 0.429 m — it can't settle. Compensating the thrust for ground effect holds the altitude error to 0.0 m.

4

Push without tumbling

Safety & guarantees

Sometimes the task needs the arm to push — press a switch, brace against a wall, seat a connector. The wall pushes back with exactly that force, and it lands on a flying body with nothing to brace against. A stiff controller that insists on its commanded position just cranks the force up until the reaction throws the whole aircraft off.

Yield with an admittance controller during contact; stiff position control fights the wall, spikes the contact force, and the reaction throws the flying body off.
peak contact force — stiff235 N (spike)
peak contact force — admittance0.6 N
wall stiffness6000 N/m

Commanding the arm 5 cm into a wall of stiffness 6000 N/m, stiff position control spikes the contact force to 235 N — enough to tumble the drone. Admittance control bounds it to 0.6 N.

5

Hold the catch

Manipulation

The instant the gripper closes on the payload, the aircraft is suddenly heavier and its balance shifts. Keep flying on the thrust that hovered the bare drone and it is now under-lifted — it sags out of the sky and drops what it just caught.

Re-trim thrust and allocation the instant the gripper closes on a payload; holding the pre-grasp hover thrust leaves the aircraft under-lifted and it sinks.
altitude drop — hold thrust0.497 m (sinks)
altitude drop — retrimmed0.015 m
added payload weight4.9 N

Grabbing a 0.5 kg payload adds 4.9 N of weight. Holding the pre-grasp hover thrust sinks the drone 0.497 m in one second. Re-trimming thrust and allocation holds the drop to 0.015 m.
