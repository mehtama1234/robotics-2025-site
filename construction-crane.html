Placing the beam

An autonomous construction crane picks a steel beam and sets it in place: stay inside the load chart so it never tips, pre-hoist for the cable that stretches, offset the trolley to beat the wind, share a tandem lift so neither crane overloads, and never two-block the hook into the boom tip. Five stages, five distinct sub-fields — each a plain rule, run and measured, with the failure it prevents.

1

Stay within the load chart

Safety & guarantees

The crane's stability is a trade between how heavy the load is and how far out it reaches. Every crane has a rated moment — a load chart — that says how far it can reach at a given weight. Reach out past that line and the load moment overpowers the counterweight: the machine tips over.

Keep the load moment (load times radius) under the crane's rated moment; reaching out past the rated radius tips the machine over.
load moment — naive reach400 kN·m (tips)
load moment — limited300 kN·m
max safe radius3 m

Rated at 300 kN·m, lifting 100 kN, the max safe radius is 3 m. A naive reach to 4 m makes a 400 kN·m moment — over the line, the crane tips. The limiter caps it at 300 kN·m.

2

Pre-hoist for the stretch

Hardware

The hoist rope is a long steel cable, and steel stretches. When the beam's full weight comes onto the hook, the rope lengthens elastically and the hook sinks. Lower to the mark with no slack taken up and the beam lands low, below the setting point.

Pre-hoist by the cable's elastic stretch before setting the load; the hoist rope lengthens when the weight comes on, so an uncompensated pick lands the beam low.
height error — uncompensated12 cm low
height error — pre-hoisted0 cm
cable stretch12 cm

Under 100 kN on a 30 m rope, the cable stretches 12 cm. Uncompensated, the beam lands 12 cm low. Pre-hoisting by the stretch lands it on target — 0 cm error.

3

Beat the wind drift

Control & MPC

The beam hangs from a long rope and a crosswind pushes on it. The load swings out until the rope's angle balances the wind, settling at a steady offset downwind. Lower it straight down from the trolley and it lands that far off the mark.

Offset the trolley upwind by the steady pendulum drift; a crosswind pushes the suspended load to a lasting offset, so a naive placement lands it downwind of the mark.
placement error — naive30 cm off
placement error — wind-compensated0 cm
wind drift30 cm

A 1 kN wind on a 30 m hang drifts a 100 kN beam 30 cm downwind. Placed naively it lands 30 cm off; offsetting the trolley upwind lands it on target — 0 cm.

4

Share the load

Safety & guarantees

One beam is too long for a single crane, so two lift it together. Its centre of mass is off-centre between the pick points. Assume each crane takes half and you hide that the crane nearer the CoM actually carries more — enough to overload it while the load chart still looks fine.

Split a tandem lift by the lever arms to the load's centre of mass, not 50/50; the crane nearer the CoM carries more, and a blind even split can overload it.
heavier crane — naive (50/50)0.80 util
heavier crane — true1.28 util (overload)
heavier crane — balanced0.80 util

Two cranes rated 100 kN lift a 160 kN beam with its CoM 3 m off-centre. A 50/50 assumption reads 0.80 utilization, but the heavier crane truly carries 128 kN — 1.28, an overload. Rebalancing brings both to 0.80.

5

Never two-block

Safety & guarantees

'Two-blocking' is hoisting the hook block up until it jams into the boom tip. The winch keeps pulling, there is nowhere left to go, and the load line snaps — dropping whatever it holds. A naive full hoist runs straight into it.

Stop the hoist at a positive hook-to-tip clearance (anti-two-block); running the hook block all the way up into the boom tip crushes it and snaps the cable.
hook-to-tip clearance — naive-1.2 m (two-blocks)
hook-to-tip clearance — limited1.0 m
safe clearance1.0 m

With the boom tip at 40 m and a 1.2 m hook block, a naive full hoist drives the clearance to -1.2 m — negative, it two-blocks. The anti-two-block limit stops it with 1.0 m of clearance.
