The big picture — connecting the dots

Strip away the subfields and every robot runs the same loop, over and over, fast: sense the world, decide what to do, move — and do it cheaply, safely, and reliably enough to trust around people. Each theme below is one slice of that loop. Read together, the same few forces show up everywhere at once.

The loop, and where the themes sit:

  • Sense — turn raw sensor streams into a usable picture of the world: self-driving perception, Gaussian-Splatting scene maps, world models that predict what happens next.
  • Decide — turn a goal into a plan: language-model planners, vision-language-action policies, multi-robot coordination.
  • Move — turn a plan into physical action that survives contact with reality: real-time motion planning, legged locomotion, grasping & dexterous hands.
  • Cutting across all three — making it cheap (cheaper training data) and making it safe (attacks, formal guarantees, knowing what you don't know).

The shifts that show up in every theme this year:

  • Learning is eating hand-engineering. Hand-coded controllers, planners, and perception pipelines are being replaced by learned ones — everywhere.
  • Foundation models arrived. The same big vision/language models trained on the internet are now the planner, the perception front-end, and the instruction-reader — across driving, planning, VLA, multi-robot, and grasping.
  • The bottleneck moved to data and reality. The hard part is no longer the algorithm but getting real experience cheaply (simulation, generative data, human video) and closing the gap between the simulator and the messy real world.
  • Efficiency became a first-class goal. Sparse representations, compression, real-time control — because a method that needs a server-grade GPU is useless on a battery-powered robot.
  • Capability outran trust. The more flexible and learned a system is, the harder it is to guarantee it won't do something dangerous — which is why safety is now its own theme, not a footnote.

The throughline: robotics in 2025 is the field learning to swap brittle, hand-built pieces for flexible learned ones — while fighting to keep them cheap enough to run, honest enough to trust, and grounded enough to survive the real world. The themes below are the specific battlegrounds; each opens with this same "big picture" treatment, then drills down to the real, cloned-and-reviewed code.
