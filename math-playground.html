optimization · translation 1

Gradient descent — the step size is everything

Roll downhill on a bowl by repeatedly stepping against the slope: x ← x − η·∇f(x). The one knob is the step size η. Too small and it crawls; too large and it overshoots the bottom and can spiral out. There is a sharp threshold — watch for it.

generative · translation 4

The score walk — noise into one real mode

Start from noise and step along the score (the arrow toward denser data) while lowering the noise level σ. With enough steps the sample lands cleanly in one of two modes — never the average between them. Too few steps and it stalls in the empty middle.

control · translation 4

Feedback control — gain, damping, and ringing

A mass tracks a target under the law u = −K·e − B·ṷ (position error e, its rate). Raise K and it reacts harder; too little damping B and it overshoots and rings; too much K with too little B and it oscillates for a long time. Hit it with a shove and watch it recover.

safety · translation 4

The safety filter — how hard to brake at the edge

A robot drives straight for a goal, but a control-barrier filter bends the command the least amount so ṁ ≥ −α·h holds (h = distance past the obstacle). Small α = cautious, wide berth; large α = it cuts the corner close. Turn α up until it grazes the edge.

attention · the softmax

Attention temperature — sharp focus or soft blur

A token looks at a handful of others, each with a match score. Softmax turns the scores into weights that sum to one: wi = esi/T / Σj esj/T. The temperature T is the one knob. Cool it toward zero and all the weight snaps onto the single best match; heat it up and the weights flatten until every key gets an equal, useless share.

estimation · sensor fusion

Kalman fusion — how much to trust the sensor

The true value drifts; a noisy sensor reports it. The filter blends its own prediction with each measurement: estimate ← prediction + K·(measurement − prediction), with gain K = σ² / (σ² + R). Noisy sensor (large R) → small K → it leans on the smooth model and lags a little. Clean sensor → K near 1 → it chases every jitter. Watch the teal line find the truth between the two.

legged balance · the capture point

The capture point — where to step to not fall

A shoved body is an inverted pendulum pivoting on its foot. There is exactly one place to plant the next foot that brings it to a dead stop: the capture point ξ = x + Ẋ/ω, where ω = √(g/L) sets the fall speed. If ξ lands inside the foot you already have, the ankle alone holds you; if it lies past how far your leg can reach, the step falls short and you topple. Shove it and see where it must step.
