Behavior cloning vs DAgger

Why copying an expert's demonstrations isn't enough — and how querying the expert on the learner's own mistakes fixes it.

home · the build lab · the method zoos · RL & imitation

Behavior cloning trains a policy by regression on an expert's (state, action) pairs. The catch: it only ever sees the expert's own near-perfect states. At run time its small residual errors carry the agent into states the expert never demonstrated, where the policy has no idea what to do — and those errors compound. DAgger fixes the distribution gap: roll out the current learner, ask the expert for the correct action on the states the learner actually visits, aggregate, and retrain.

we ran itOn a car following a curvy lane in a crosswind, behavior cloning fits the expert well (train MSE 0.43) yet drifts a mean 0.61 off the lane closed-loop (max 12.0) — a memoryless copy can't reproduce the expert's wind-cancelling integral term, so its small steady error integrates into heading and compounds. DAgger, labelling the learner's own visited states over 6 iterations, holds a mean 0.045 off the lane (max 0.35) — 14× tighter.
the failure it shows The compounding-error / distribution-shift failure of imitation learning: a policy trained only on the expert's states leaves that distribution the moment it makes a small mistake, and the error snowballs.
