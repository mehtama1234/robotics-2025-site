autonomous excavator · the dig cycle, stage by stage

Moving earth

An autonomous excavator runs a dig-and-dump cycle over and over: survey how much to move, dig without spinning its tracks, weigh each scoop, swing the load to the truck without spilling, and grade the finished ground flat. Five stages, five distinct sub-fields — each a plain rule, run and measured, with the failure it prevents.

Each stage is one link in the dig cycle: know the volume, put force into the ground not the tracks, weigh the load, land it in the truck, and leave the site on grade.

1

Survey the dig site

3D perception

Before it digs, the excavator scans the site into a height map and computes how much earth to move. A single laser scan is noisy, and the noise on the flat, at-grade ground counts as material to remove — so the earthwork estimate that the whole job is bid and planned against comes out systematically too high.

Average scans before computing cut volume; rectifying a single noisy height map counts noise on flat ground as material and overestimates the earthwork.
volume error — single scan+11%
volume error — averaged+2%
true earthwork621 m³

Against a true earthwork of 621 m³, a single noisy scan estimates 690 m³ (+11%) — the rectification bias. Averaging 20 scans lands at 637 m³ (+2%).

2

Regulate the dig force

Control & MPC

To fill the bucket the machine pushes it into the pile — but it can only push as hard as its tracks grip the ground. Command more dig force than the tracks can hold and they break loose: the machine spins its tracks and slides backward, digging nothing.

Regulate the dig force to the track traction limit; commanding more force than the tracks can hold just spins them and slides the machine back.
penetration — over the limit0.0 m (slips)
penetration — regulated1.84 m
traction limit100 kN

With a 100 kN traction limit, commanding 140 kN slips the tracks 100% of the time and advances the bucket 0.0 m. Regulating to 90 kN — under the limit — digs 1.84 m.

3

Weigh the bucket

Hardware

Every scoop needs to be weighed — for load tickets, for not overloading the truck, for knowing when the truck is full. Assume each bucket comes up full and you are wrong by a quarter of a bucket every time, because scoops vary from half to full.

Weigh the bucket from the lift dynamics; assuming every scoop comes up full mis-bills the load by a quarter of a bucket.
load error — assume full589 kg (29%)
load error — lift dynamics8 kg (0%)
bucket capacity2000 kg

Across scoops from half to full, assuming a full 2000 kg bucket mis-weighs the load by 589 kg (29%). Estimating from the lift torque cuts the error to 8 kg (0%).

4

Swing without spilling

Control & MPC

The loaded bucket hangs from the boom and swings like a pendulum. Slew the boom to the truck with a blunt accelerate-then-stop and the load is still swinging when you get there — it slops over the sides and misses the bed.

Input-shape the swing command (two half-moves a half-period apart); a bang-bang swing leaves the hung load swinging and it spills.
residual sway — bang-bang0.075 rad
residual sway — input-shaped0.0 rad
sway period4.9 s

On a 6 m hang (4.9 s sway period), a bang-bang slew leaves 0.075 rad of residual sway. The input-shaped slew leaves 0.0 rad — the load stops when the boom does.

5

Grade the surface

Navigation

The final pass has to leave the ground flat to a target grade. Hold the blade at a fixed height off the chassis and every bump the machine climbs pitches the blade up and down — the 'finished' surface is a copy of the rough ground the machine drove over.

Close the loop on blade height to a laser/GPS grade reference; an open-loop blade copies every machine pitch into the finished surface.
surface roughness — open-loop blade8.2 cm
surface roughness — grade control0.5 cm

Over rough ground, an open-loop blade leaves a finished surface with 8.2 cm of roughness — it copies the machine's pitch. Grade control on a fixed reference brings it to 0.5 cm.
