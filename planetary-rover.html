1

Trust the ground, not the wheels

3D perception

The rover has to know how far it has driven — to hit waypoints, to hand back a map, to not overshoot a target. Its wheels turn in loose Martian regolith, and the regolith slips: the wheels spin farther than the rover actually moves. Count the wheel turns and the odometry insists the rover has gone farther than it has.

Trust visual odometry over the wheels; in slipping regolith the wheels turn farther than the rover moves, so wheel odometry over-reports distance while tracking terrain features measures the true traverse.
distance error — wheel odometry+43%
distance error — visual odometry-0%
slip ratio30%

Over a true 100 m traverse at a 30% slip ratio, wheel odometry reports 143 m (+43%) — it over-reports by the slip. Visual odometry lands at 100 m (-0%).

2

Drive without a joystick

World models

Mars is roughly a 20-minute round-trip away at light speed. Drive it by joystick from Earth and every command — turn, roll forward, stop — costs a full round-trip before the driver even sees what happened. A whole sol of driving becomes a handful of short nudges, and the rover barely moves.

Navigate on-board, not by joystick from Earth; with a 20-minute round-trip a teleoperated rover gets one command per trip and crawls, while continuous on-board autonomy covers far more ground per sol.
distance per sol — teleop36 m
distance per sol — autonomous864 m
round-trip delay20 min

With a 20-minute round-trip over a 360-minute window, teleoperation covers 36 m per sol. On-board autonomy drives continuously and covers 864 m — about 24× farther.

3

Read the terrain

Navigation

The straight line to the next target runs through whatever is in the way — a 30° slope, a rock taller than the wheels. Drive it blind and the rover high-centres on a boulder or slides on a scarp: it gets stuck, or it gets damaged, and either way the mission stalls.

Plan on a traversability map, not a straight line; a straight path crosses steep, rocky cells and the rover gets stuck or damaged, while a slope-and-roughness-aware planner routes around every hazard for only a modest extra distance.
hazards crossed — straight line2
hazards crossed — terrain-aware0
path length — aware vs straight79 vs 40 cells

On a 40×40 terrain map with 64 untraversable cells, a straight-line path crosses 2 of them. A traversability-aware planner crosses 0 — routing around every hazard for a path of 79 cells vs the straight 40.

4

Budget the sun

Safety & guarantees

Every joule the rover spends comes from a solar panel and a small battery, and both run out. Drive continuously and the battery drains straight through the first night — the rover freezes in shadow with a dead battery and can't warm itself enough to recharge. That is a lost rover.

Schedule driving inside the daily energy budget and park in the sun; a rover that drives continuously drains its battery into shadow and freezes, while a power-aware schedule survives every night and still makes progress.
naive continuous drive560 m, STRANDED
power-aware schedule10220 m, survives
battery capacity500 Wh

On a 500 Wh battery, a naive continuous drive covers 560 m and then strands — battery to 0 Wh, frozen in shadow. A power-aware schedule drives 10220 m over the same span and survives, ending with 120 Wh in the bank.

5

Don't get stuck

Control & MPC

In soft sand the wheels sink, and the deeper they sink the more they slip instead of roll. Past a threshold the wheels are churning in place — the rover embeds and is stuck for good, the way Spirit ended its mission. Push on through a soft patch regardless and that is exactly where you end up.

Monitor the slip ratio and back out when it spikes; in soft sand the wheels sink and slip climbs until the rover embeds and is stuck for good, so stopping on a slip spike keeps the rover mobile while pushing on regardless embeds it.
aggressive driveembeds at 88% slip
slip-monitored drivemobile, 61% slip
embed threshold85% slip

Driving aggressively through a sand trap, slip climbs to 88% and the rover embeds after 110 m — immobile. Slip-monitored driving caps slip at 61%, backs out, and stays mobile after 106 m.
