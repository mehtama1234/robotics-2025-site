autonomous-driving stack · the pipeline, stage by stage

Driving the road

A self-driving car has to see the vehicle ahead, keep a stable track on it through clutter, forecast where it is going, hold a gap that survives a hard brake, and steer its own lane without weaving. Five stages, five distinct sub-fields — each a plain rule, run and measured, with the failure it prevents. A production narrative, not a grab-bag: each stage feeds the next.

Each stage is one link: the fused detection feeds the tracker, the track feeds the predictor, the prediction feeds the gap-keeper, and the plan is driven by the lane controller. A weak link anywhere and the car mislocates, loses the target, mis-forecasts, tailgates, or weaves.

1

Detect the vehicle

Autonomous driving

The car has a camera and a radar looking at the same vehicle ahead, and they disagree. The camera nails the bearing but is vague on distance; the radar nails the distance but smears the bearing. Trust either one alone and you get a position that is wrong in one axis.

Fuse sensors by their noise: a camera pins the bearing, a radar pins the range, and inverse-covariance fusion is sharp in both.
RMSE — camera alone3.02 m
RMSE — radar alone3.03 m
RMSE — fused0.69 m

On its own the camera localises the vehicle to 3.02 m RMSE and the radar to 3.03 m — each crippled by its bad axis. Inverse-covariance fusion brings both together to 0.69 m, about 4.4× tighter than either sensor.

2

Track it over time

Video & temporal

Frame to frame, the detector returns the real vehicle plus a scatter of false blips off guardrails and reflections — and every so often it misses the real one entirely. Snap the track to the nearest blip each frame and it leaps onto clutter the moment the true detection drops.

Gate every detection against the track's own prediction; an ungated tracker lets clutter hijack the target.
RMSE — no gating2.5 m
RMSE — with gating0.47 m
clutter per frame · detection rate3 · 70%

With 3 clutter blips per frame and the true detection present only 70% of the time, an ungated tracker drifts to 2.5 m RMSE as it snaps onto clutter. Gating against the prediction holds it to 0.47 m — 5.3× tighter.

3

Predict where it goes

Autonomous driving

To plan around another car you need its next three seconds. Extrapolate its current velocity in a straight line and, the instant that car is turning through a bend, your forecast shoots off the road while the car calmly follows the curve.

Predict with the motion the object is actually doing: a constant-velocity guess leaves the road the moment the car is turning.
3 s error — constant velocity13.65 m
3 s error — constant turn rate0.45 m
prediction horizon3 s

For a car cornering at a steady yaw rate, a straight-line constant-velocity forecast is off by 13.65 m at the 3-second horizon — most of a lane-width and then some. A constant-turn-rate model predicts to 0.45 m.

4

Keep a safe gap

Safety & guarantees

Following a car at highway speed, a comfortable-looking fixed gap of a couple of metres feels fine — until the lead slams on its brakes. Between your reaction time and your own braking distance, a fixed gap is nowhere near enough and you rear-end it.

Keep a following gap that grows with speed (reaction + braking distance); a fixed gap rear-ends a hard-braking lead.
min gap — fixed 2 m-26.0 m (crash)
safe gap — speed-dependent30.0 m
min gap — safe policy2.0 m (clear)

When the lead brakes hard from 25 m/s, a fixed 2 m gap collapses to -26.0 m — a 26 m overlap, a crash. The speed-dependent safe gap of 30.0 m bottoms out at 2.0 m — no contact.

5

Hold the lane

Control & MPC

The car sits half a lane off the centreline and has to ease back on. Steer in proportion to how far off you are and nothing else, and the car arrives at the line still pointed across it, sails past, and weaves back and forth — a seasick lane-keeper.

Steer on heading error plus cross-track (Stanley), not cross-track alone, or lane-keeping overshoots and weaves.
overshoot — cross-track only2.0 m
overshoot — Stanley0.08 m
residual weave — P-only vs Stanley1.389 m vs 0.0 m

Starting 2 m off the centreline, a cross-track-only controller overshoots the line by 2.0 m and never settles (1.389 m residual weave). Adding the heading term overshoots just 0.08 m and settles to 0.0 m.
