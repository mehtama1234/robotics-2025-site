safe-deploy · from sim to a trusted robot

Deploying a learned policy safely

A learned policy that works in simulation is not ready for a real robot. Four safeguards stand between the two — a lifecycle. Each is a plain rule, and each is run and measured on one honest point-robot simulation (a robot steering to a goal past an obstacle). Built in the demo-package style of the CV project: a tested, reusable core behind every stage.

The same failure runs through every stage: a policy that looks finished in sim, trusted blindly, breaks in the world. The lifecycle is how you stop trusting it blindly.

1 gate → 2 shield → 3 detect → 4 recover  ·  hold what isn't ready, filter every action, watch for the unknown, and fall back when it fails.
1

Deploy Readiness Gate

A policy that scores well in the sim it was tuned on can fail the instant conditions shift. Signing off on the training score is how a system that looks finished goes to production and breaks.

Judge a policy on held-out cases, not the region it was recorded from.
recorded demo — train region86%
recorded demo — held-out0%
feedback policy — held-out100%
gate threshold85%

The recorded-demo policy scores 86% in the region it was recorded from but 0% on held-out starts, so the gate holds it. A feedback policy scores 100% held-out and is cleared to deploy.

2

Runtime Safety Shield

A learned controller will, now and then, command an action that drives the system into something it must not hit. You cannot re-train the fear of collisions into it fast enough for production.

Gate every learned action through a filter that can only make it safer.
collision rate — no shield100%
collision rate — with shield0%
goal reached — with shield68%

With the shield off the unsafe policy collides on 100% of runs; with the shield on, collisions drop to 0% while it still reaches the goal 68% of the time.

3

Out-of-Regime Failure Detector

A model is only trustworthy on inputs like the ones it learned from. In production it will meet inputs from outside that regime and answer them with the same false confidence.

Watch for leaving the trained regime; an honest 'unsure' beats a confident wrong action.
out-of-regime caught (recall)100%
false alarms on in-regime3%

On a stream of in-regime and out-of-regime states the detector flags 100% of the out-of-regime cases while false-alarming on only 3% of in-regime ones.

4

Detect-and-Recover Fallback

When a policy is failing, the worst thing it can do is keep going. A silent, confident failure that continues to command actuators is how a recoverable problem becomes damage.

On a detected failure, fall back to a safe default; don't push a failing policy.
crash rate — policy alone100%
crash rate — with fallback0%
safe aborts100%

On held-out deployments the policy alone crashes 100% of the time; with detect-and-recover wired in, crashes fall to 0% — every failing run becomes a flagged, safe abort (100%).

How this was built. Real point-robot experiments (scripts/safe_deploy_experiments.py, pure NumPy) produce the numbers above. Each stage is also a small tested demo package under source-code/learning/safe-deploy/<stage>/ — a reusable core.js carrying the rule, with node assert-tests — so the insight is not just a figure but a primitive you can drop onto your own eval logs. This mirrors the CV project's coverage + quality discipline: every concept a runnable, tested, decision-rule demo.
