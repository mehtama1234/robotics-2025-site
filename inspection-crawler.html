in-pipe inspection crawler · the inspection run, stage by stage

Inside the pipe

An in-pipe inspection crawler drives a pipeline from the inside and reports its condition: it localizes by counting girth welds, flags real corrosion instead of noise, grips the wall on steep inclines, covers the full circumference, and localizes leaks. Five stages, five distinct sub-fields — each a plain rule, run and measured, with the failure it prevents.

Each stage is one link in the inspection run: know where you are, tell defects from noise, hold to the wall, see all of it, and pin down a leak.

1

Count the welds

Navigation

Inside a long, featureless pipe there are no GPS and no landmarks — the crawler tracks how far it has travelled from its wheels alone. But the wheels slip against the wall, so the distance count drifts a little further off with every metre, and by the far end the robot has no idea where the corrosion it just logged actually is.

Reset wheel odometry to the known girth-weld locations; unaided odometry accumulates the wheel-slip fraction over the whole run and loses track of where the crawler is.
position error — odometry only15.2 m
position error — weld-corrected0.00 m
weld spacing12 m

Over a 300 m pipe with a 5% wheel-slip, unaided odometry ends up 15.2 m off — a whole pipe section of uncertainty. Resetting at each weld (every 12 m) ends the run within 0.00 m.

2

Flag real corrosion

3D perception

The crawler measures the wall thickness with an ultrasonic probe to catch corrosion thinning the pipe. The readings are noisy, so a plain 'below the threshold → flag it' rule fires constantly on measurement noise — a wall of false alarms an inspector has to hand-check, and the real defects hide in the flood.

Require several adjacent readings below the threshold before flagging corrosion; a single-reading threshold turns ultrasonic noise into false alarms, while real corrosion is spatially extended and survives the consistency test.
false-alarm rate — single reading13.5%
false-alarm rate — spatially consistent0.05%
corrosion recall — consistent99%

A single-reading threshold false-alarms on 13.5% of good wall. Requiring 4 adjacent readings below threshold drops that to 0.05% — while recall of the real corrosion patches holds at 99% (vs 99%).

3

Grip the pipe wall

Hardware

Pipelines run uphill, downhill and vertically. The crawler holds to the wall by pressing its wheels against it — friction times that press-force has to beat the pull of gravity along the pipe. A press-force tuned on flat pipe is far too weak on a steep riser: the wheels slip and the crawler slides back down.

Raise the wheel normal force with the incline; a constant grip tuned on the flat cannot hold the crawler's weight on the steep section and it slides back.
slip fraction — constant grip51%
slip fraction — adaptive grip0%
max incline75°

On a route with a 75° section, a constant flat-tuned grip slips 51% of the route. Raising the normal force with the incline holds everywhere — slip drops to 0%.

4

Cover the whole wall

Navigation

To certify a pipe you have to inspect the whole wall, all the way around. The sensor only sees a narrow swath of the circumference at a time. Drive straight down the axis and you inspect one thin stripe of the wall and declare the pipe clear — while a defect a few degrees away around the bore never comes under the sensor.

Drive a helical path so consecutive turns tile the full circumference; a straight axial pass only inspects the strip the sensor swath covers and leaves most of the wall unseen.
wall coverage — straight pass4%
wall coverage — helical100%
sensor swath8 cm

With an 8 cm swath on a 0.6 m pipe (1.89 m around), a straight axial pass covers just 4% of the wall. A helical path covers 100%.

5

Find the leak

Safety & guarantees

A leak makes a pressure and acoustic signature that fades with distance along the pipe. A single sensor picks up the signature and can tell you a leak exists — but the fall-off is symmetric, so the same reading fits a small leak nearby or a big one far away, in either direction. You know there's a leak; you don't know where to dig.

Localize a leak by triangulating from several sensors along the pipe; a single sensor detects that a leak exists but the symmetric distance fall-off leaves its position undetermined.
leak-location error — single sensor~50 m
leak-location error — multi-sensor0.2 m
sensors in array10

On a 100 m run, a single sensor leaves the leak position undetermined to about ±50 m — half the pipe. Triangulating from 10 sensors along the pipe locates it to within 0.2 m.
